# Welcome to FlowAnRiver

How many times can we reset in one lifetime? I've asked myself that question more than once.

Maybe it's the Gemini in me, but I've always been someone who enjoys exploring new ideas, trying different things, and occasionally changing direction. Life hasn't always gone the way I planned, and I've had to start over more than once. Somewhere along the way, though, I stopped being so afraid of starting again.

Of course, I have my difficult moments. I get frustrated, disappointed, and sometimes just want to be left alone for a while. But I've learned how to find my own way back. Sometimes it's through reading, painting, going somewhere, or simply spending time with myself. I don't always need someone else to make me feel better.

Over the years, I've worked with content, explored different business ideas, and tried to build things of my own. Some worked, others didn't. Looking back, I don't think every unfinished plan was a failure. Sometimes I learned something valuable, and sometimes I simply realized that what I once wanted wasn't what I wanted anymore.

AI has opened up possibilities I once thought were beyond my abilities. I'm gradually learning to work across different fields, even though I still have plenty to learn. Ideas that used to stay in my head now feel worth trying. I may not know how to do everything, but with AI, I'm much more willing to experiment, build, make mistakes, and figure things out along the way.

AI hasn't replaced my own thinking, though. I've learned to question its answers, recognize its limitations, and make my own decisions. What I appreciate most is that it has made the distance between an idea and actually trying it feel much smaller. Without it, quite a few of my projects might still be sitting somewhere in my imagination.

Crypto has taught me something different. It has brought me closer to understanding how finance actually works. The more I learn, the more I realize that it's not simply about prices going up or down, or the dream of getting rich overnight. What interests me is how financial systems develop, how money moves, how regulations take shape, and where blockchain and digital assets might fit into that larger picture.

Having worked on my own e-commerce business, I've experienced some of the practical challenges of international payments and operating across platforms. That experience has made me especially curious about whether new financial technologies can solve real problems, rather than simply create another wave of excitement.

But technology and business are only part of my world.

I've always enjoyed culture, art, books, and observing people. Human nature fascinates me, especially the choices we make, the way we respond to change, and how differently we can see the same situation. Sometimes a conversation, an old animation, or an ordinary afternoon gives me something to think about long after the moment has passed.

That's why FlowAnRiver isn't limited to one subject.

It's where I write about what I'm learning, what I'm questioning, and whatever catches my attention along the way. Some articles come from my work or the projects I'm building. Others come from everyday experiences, memories, or thoughts I simply want to put into words.

I still have plenty of ideas I want to try, places I'd love to visit, and things I haven't figured out yet. Perhaps there will be another reset somewhere along the way. Who knows? That's life.

Everything I write on FlowAnRiver comes from the way I see and understand the world. Some of my thoughts may be questioned, and others may see things differently. That's perfectly fine with me. I'm not here to convince everyone to agree with me. These are simply my personal views, shaped by my own experiences and understanding.
