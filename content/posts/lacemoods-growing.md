Recently, I redefined LaceMoods and updated the website. I wouldn’t really call it a redesign. It feels more like I finally started bringing together all the scattered ideas I’ve had over the past few years and giving them a place to belong.

LaceMoods started in a very simple way. It began with women’s lingerie.

I’ve always liked lace, soft fabrics, and the feeling that comes with wearing something beautiful, even when nobody else sees it. Lingerie is still one of those things people can feel slightly awkward talking about, and it often gets linked to sexiness almost immediately. But if you strip away all those assumptions, underwear is simply one of the most intimate pieces of clothing in a woman’s life. From the time a girl starts growing up, it becomes something she wears almost every day and something that stays physically close to her body.

Of course, lingerie can be beautiful, sexy, playful or bold, but that does not mean it has to be for someone else. A woman can enjoy it for herself. She can care about how it feels, how it fits, or simply how it makes her feel when she looks in the mirror. Even sexiness does not always have to be about being seen or desired. Sometimes, it is just yours.

That idea has always been part of LaceMoods for me.

LaceMoods can be a beautiful bra or a piece of sleepwear you put on after a shower. It can also be a scent in your room, a small gift, or a bunch of flowers you buy for yourself for no particular reason. These ordinary, almost forgettable details are often the things that quietly shape how a day feels, and that has always been closer to what I wanted LaceMoods to mean.

As I kept working on the brand, though, I realized that what I wanted to talk about was no longer limited to lingerie. I became more interested in scent, gifts, flowers and small objects for everyday life, but I also became increasingly curious about how products are actually made — how something that exists as an idea slowly becomes something real that you can hold in your hands.

At the same time, my own work changed.

Over the past few years, I started doing more B2B sourcing, speaking with factories, finding suppliers, reviewing samples, discussing packaging and materials, working through MOQ requirements and looking at what different markets expect from a product. I also found myself helping other people take an idea that existed only in their head and move it closer to production.

For a while, I treated that as a completely separate part of my work. Then I started to realize that it was not actually very far away from LaceMoods at all.

I used to think that a brand needed to answer one question very clearly: what exactly do you sell?

If you sell lingerie, then you are a lingerie brand. If you sell fragrance, then you are a fragrance brand. Gifts belong somewhere else, and sourcing should probably sit on an entirely different website.

Now I think there is another question that matters just as much: what kind of world are you trying to create?

For me, LaceMoods has never really been defined by one product. It has always been more about a feeling.

Soft, feminine, curious. Sometimes quiet, sometimes sexy, sometimes a little playful and childish in the best way.

That is why, when I updated the website, I did not remove lingerie from LaceMoods. I actually wanted to make sure it stayed.

That is where the brand began, and I do not want to erase that beginning just because the brand is growing. What I want is to give it more space. Lingerie can sit next to fragrance, gifts, flowers, playful objects and things I have not even thought of yet, because all of them can carry the same idea: a woman creating small moments of care for herself.

A woman can be playful. She can be calm and composed. She can be a mother, a girlfriend, a wife, or simply herself. She can love someone deeply without making love the only thing that defines her. Different parts of us can exist at the same time, and I think that is something I want LaceMoods to reflect.

One line on the new website captures this better than a product category ever could:

**Everyday Objects. A World of Moods.**

That feels much closer to what I actually want to build.

![The redesigned LaceMoods homepage, reflecting its shift from lingerie into a broader lifestyle and product creation brand.](/images/lacemoods-new-lifestyle-homepage.png "lacemoods-new-lifestyle-homepage")

Life is mostly made up of ordinary things — clothes, scent, a lamp, a small present for a friend, or even an object that serves no practical purpose at all but makes you happy every time you see it. The product itself may be ordinary, but the feeling it creates does not have to be.

This is also why I added another part to LaceMoods: **For Brands**.

Alongside creating products of our own, I also want to help other brands make theirs.

China has an incredibly developed supply chain, but finding a supplier and finding the right supplier are very different things. Price matters, of course, but so do MOQ, materials, certifications, samples, packaging, communication, production experience and whether a factory has actually made the kind of product you are trying to create.

These details can look small on paper, but they often decide whether a good idea becomes a good product or turns into a frustrating project.

This is work I have already been doing for years, so instead of keeping it hidden under another professional identity, I decided to let it become part of LaceMoods too.

We create our own products, and we help other people create theirs.

To me, those two things do not compete with each other. In fact, being on the brand side myself makes me understand other founders better. I know what it feels like to worry about packaging, minimum quantities, materials, samples and whether the final product will actually look and feel like the thing you imagined in the beginning.

LaceMoods will probably continue to change.

In the past, I might have taken that as a sign that the original positioning was wrong. I do not see it that way anymore. Brands grow, just like people do. Changing does not automatically mean rejecting what came before, and growing into a larger market does not mean throwing away the things that mattered at the beginning.

I still want LaceMoods to feel soft.

I still love lace, scent, flowers, and those slightly unnecessary things that somehow make daily life feel nicer. I hope that even if LaceMoods becomes much bigger one day, it keeps a little of that feeling.

That we do not forget the girl we used to be.

We grow up. We work. Some of us become mothers. We deal with bills, contracts, logistics, factories, supply chains and all the practical parts of life that nobody really romanticizes. But growing up does not mean that softness has to disappear.

Maybe real growth is not about leaving that part of yourself behind. Maybe it is about finally becoming strong enough to keep a place for it.

So I do not really see this as a rebranding.

I see it as growing.

LaceMoods simply has more room now — room for products, ideas, softness, business, creativity, and the practical work it takes to bring something into the world.

I do not know exactly what LaceMoods will become in the future.

But for the first time, it feels much closer to the LaceMoods I had in my head all along.
